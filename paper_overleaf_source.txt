% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves the factual grounding of
large language models by incorporating information retrieved from
external knowledge sources. However, retrieval errors, insufficient
evidence, and unsupported generation can still produce unreliable
responses. Recent approaches have introduced adaptive retrieval,
self-reflection, corrective retrieval, and iterative self-correction to
address these limitations. Nevertheless, generating a new response does
not necessarily guarantee improved grounding over the previous response.

This work proposes VBA-SCRAG (Verify-before-Accept Self-Correcting
Retrieval-Augmented Generation), a reliability-aware framework that
introduces an explicit verification gate into the correction loop. The
framework evaluates the grounding of an initial response, performs a
targeted corrective action when required, and verifies the candidate
response before accepting it. A correction is accepted only when its
grounding score improves over the previous response by at least a
predefined improvement margin; otherwise, the previous response is
retained.

A preliminary evaluation is conducted using 50 questions from the
HotpotQA validation dataset. Vanilla RAG, Self-Correcting RAG without
the verification gate, and VBA-SCRAG are compared using evidence
grounding and correction-related metrics. The three configurations
achieved average final grounding scores of 0.347978, 0.334418, and
0.354460, respectively. VBA-SCRAG achieved an average improvement of
0.006482 over the initial grounding score. Among 45 correction attempts,
37 were accepted and 8 were rejected, resulting in acceptance and
rejection rates of 82.22\% and 17.78\%, respectively. These preliminary
results demonstrate the operation of the verify-before-accept mechanism
and provide a basis for further evaluation on larger and more diverse
datasets.
\end{abstract}

% I. INTRODUCTION
% ============================================================

\section{Introduction}

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in
natural language understanding and generation across a wide range of
tasks. However, their responses may contain unsupported, inaccurate, or
hallucinated information, particularly when answering questions that
require knowledge beyond the information reliably encoded in the model.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by
retrieving information from an external knowledge source and providing
the retrieved evidence as context to the language model during answer
generation.

A conventional RAG pipeline generally consists of query processing,
document retrieval, context construction, and response generation.
Although this approach can improve factual grounding, the quality of
the generated response remains dependent on the quality and sufficiency
of the retrieved evidence. An irrelevant or incomplete retrieval result
can propagate errors into the generation stage. Similarly, even when
relevant evidence is retrieved, the language model may fail to use the
evidence correctly or may generate claims that are not adequately
supported by the retrieved context.

To address these limitations, recent research has explored mechanisms
including adaptive retrieval, self-reflection, corrective retrieval,
iterative generation, and retrieval evaluation. Self-RAG introduced
self-reflection mechanisms for deciding when retrieval and critique are
required. Corrective retrieval approaches investigate whether retrieved
documents are adequate before generation. Other approaches investigate
iterative and reflective mechanisms for improving generated responses
through additional reasoning or retrieval cycles.

However, iterative correction introduces another important question:
\emph{when should a corrected response actually be accepted?} A
correction step may retrieve different evidence or generate a different
response without necessarily improving its grounding. Automatically
replacing the previous response with every newly generated response can
therefore introduce unnecessary iterations or potentially accept a
response that is not better supported by the available evidence.

This motivates a verify-before-accept principle for self-correcting RAG.
Instead of treating the completion of a correction step as sufficient
for accepting a new response, the proposed approach explicitly
evaluates whether the correction has improved the grounding of the
response. The system maintains the previous response as a reference
state and compares it with the corrected response using an
evidence-grounding verifier.

Based on this principle, this work proposes
\textbf{VBA-SCRAG (Verify-before-Accept Self-Correcting
Retrieval-Augmented Generation)}. The framework introduces a
verification gate between the correction and acceptance stages. When
the initial response does not satisfy the required grounding
threshold, the system diagnoses the potential failure and performs a
corrective action. The corrected response is subsequently evaluated,
and it is accepted only when its grounding score improves by at least a
predefined margin. If the condition is not satisfied, the correction is
rejected and the system retains the previous response.

The proposed framework therefore treats self-correction as a controlled
decision process rather than an unconditional iterative generation
process.

The main contributions of this work are:

\begin{itemize}

\item A verify-before-accept mechanism for self-correcting RAG that
explicitly evaluates a correction before replacing the current
response.

\item An evidence-grounding verification mechanism for assessing
whether a generated response is adequately supported by retrieved
evidence.

\item A bounded correction strategy that considers grounding improvement
before accepting a new response.

\item An evaluation framework covering grounding improvement,
correction acceptance, correction rejection, iteration behavior, and
computational behavior.

\item A preliminary comparison between Vanilla RAG, conventional
self-correcting RAG, and VBA-SCRAG.

\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
% II. RELATED WORK
% =========================================================

\section{Related Work}

Recent research on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has explored
adaptive retrieval, self-reflection, corrective retrieval, iterative
self-correction, evidence verification, and reliability-aware generation.
These developments provide the foundation for the proposed VBA-SCRAG
framework and motivate the need for an explicit decision mechanism for
accepting or rejecting generated corrections.

\subsection{Adaptive Retrieval and Self-Reflection}

Self-RAG introduced a self-reflective retrieval-augmented generation
framework in which a language model learns to determine when retrieval
is required and evaluates retrieved evidence and generated responses
through reflection mechanisms \cite{selfrag}. This approach
extends the conventional retrieve--generate pipeline by incorporating
retrieval and critique decisions into the generation process.

Self-BioRAG extended retrieval and self-reflection to biomedical
question answering by incorporating domain-adaptive reflection
mechanisms and a biomedical retriever \cite{selfbiorag}.
These approaches demonstrate the usefulness of feedback during
generation and show that retrieval and reflection can be adapted to
task-specific requirements.

Reflective RAG further investigates self-evaluation-driven strategy
optimization for agentic retrieval-augmented generation
\cite{reflectiverag}. Similarly, RAG-Reflect incorporates
reflection into an agentic retrieval and reasoning workflow for
comment-driven code maintenance \cite{ragreflect}. These
studies demonstrate that reflection can influence subsequent retrieval,
reasoning, and generation decisions.

However, these approaches do not necessarily provide an explicit
post-correction acceptance rule that compares a candidate response with
the previously accepted response. A newly generated response may
therefore be produced without explicitly measuring whether it provides
a measurable improvement in evidence grounding.

\subsection{Corrective and Adaptive Retrieval}

Corrective Retrieval-Augmented Generation (CRAG) introduced a retrieval
evaluator that assesses the quality of retrieved documents and triggers
corrective retrieval when the available evidence is considered
inadequate \cite{crag}. The framework combines retrieval
evaluation, corrective retrieval, and knowledge refinement, providing a
failure-aware alternative to a fixed retrieval pipeline.

TIRESRAG-R1 investigated retrieval-augmented reasoning using
reinforcement learning and identified failure conditions including
information insufficiency, faulty reasoning, and answer--reasoning
inconsistency \cite{tiresrag}. These failure categories are
relevant to the failure-diagnosis component of VBA-SCRAG.

An open-source reproduction and explainability analysis of CRAG further
investigated the behavior of corrective retrieval and its retrieval
evaluation mechanism \cite{cragreproduction}. Bidirectional RAG
also explored multi-stage validation for safer self-improving
retrieval-augmented generation \cite{bidirectional}.

These studies demonstrate the importance of evaluating retrieved
evidence and performing corrective actions when retrieval is inadequate.
However, corrective retrieval alone does not guarantee that the
subsequently generated response is better grounded than the previously
accepted response. VBA-SCRAG therefore introduces an additional
decision stage that evaluates the candidate response before replacing
the current response.

\subsection{Iterative Self-Correction}

Research on intrinsic self-correction has investigated whether large
language models can identify and correct deficiencies in their own
generated responses without requiring additional external knowledge
\cite{intrinsic}. The findings indicate that the effectiveness
of self-correction depends on the model, task, and prompting conditions.

Self-correcting RAG systems have extended this idea by combining
retrieval, contextual selection, verification, and iterative correction.
For example, Self-Correcting RAG employs MMKP-based context selection
and NLI-guided Monte Carlo Tree Search to improve the faithfulness of
generated responses \cite{selfcorrecting}.

Self-feedback has also been investigated for retrieval-augmented
generation in biomedical question answering. The BioASQ self-feedback
study examined whether language models can critique their own
retrieval-augmented responses and reported that the effectiveness of
self-feedback varies across models and tasks \cite{bioasq}.

These studies demonstrate the potential of iterative feedback and
correction. However, repeated generation may introduce additional
computational cost and may produce a candidate response that is not
better than the previous response. Therefore, an explicit mechanism is
required to determine whether the newly generated correction should be
accepted.

\subsection{Fine-Grained Evidence Verification}

Reliable RAG systems require mechanisms for determining whether
generated responses are adequately supported by retrieved evidence.
RAGChecker introduced a fine-grained evaluation framework for diagnosing
retrieval and generation quality using claim-level and entailment-based
metrics \cite{ragchecker}. Such evaluation provides a basis for
measuring evidence grounding beyond simple end-to-end answer
evaluation.

G-Retriever investigated retrieval-augmented generation for textual
graph understanding and question answering through graph-based
evidence selection \cite{gretriever}. HalluSearch investigated
retrieval-augmented hallucination detection and fine-grained
localization of unsupported content \cite{hallusearch}.
Evidence-Aligned Entity Verification further examined entity--evidence
alignment using multiple verification dimensions and counterfactual
stability tests \cite{evidenceverification}.

These approaches demonstrate that evidence-grounding signals can be
computed at different levels of granularity. However, in the context of
self-correcting RAG, the important question is not only whether a
response can be evaluated, but whether the evaluation should directly
control the acceptance of a correction.

\subsection{Research Gap}

The reviewed literature demonstrates substantial progress in adaptive
retrieval, self-reflection, corrective retrieval, iterative
self-correction, and evidence verification. However, these mechanisms
primarily address retrieval improvement, response generation,
self-evaluation, or evidence diagnosis as separate stages.

Three observations motivate the proposed framework. First, evidence
verification is often used as an evaluation or diagnostic mechanism
rather than as an explicit per-query condition for accepting a
corrected response. Second, iterative correction mechanisms may generate
a new response without explicitly measuring whether the candidate
provides a measurable grounding improvement over the previously
accepted response. Third, repeated correction introduces additional
computation, making bounded correction and controlled response
replacement important considerations.

Based on these observations, this work addresses the need for an
explicit \textit{verify-before-accept} mechanism in self-correcting RAG.
The proposed framework:

\begin{itemize}

\item measures the grounding of the initial response;

\item identifies whether corrective action is required;

\item generates a candidate correction using additional retrieval or
regeneration;

\item independently evaluates the grounding of the candidate response;

\item calculates the grounding improvement relative to the previously
accepted response; and

\item accepts the correction only when the predefined verification
condition is satisfied.

\end{itemize}

VBA-SCRAG addresses this gap by introducing a verification gate between
the correction and acceptance stages. Instead of assuming that every
correction improves the response, the framework explicitly compares the
candidate grounding score with the previous grounding score and retains
the previous response when the required improvement condition is not
satisfied.

% III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
% ============================================================

\section{Proposed Methodology}

The proposed VBA-SCRAG framework consists of five major stages:

\begin{enumerate}

\item Query processing and retrieval.

\item Initial response generation.

\item Evidence grounding evaluation.

\item Corrective retrieval and regeneration.

\item Verify-before-accept decision.

\end{enumerate}

The overall process can be represented as:

\begin{equation}
q \rightarrow R_0 \rightarrow a_0 \rightarrow g_0
\rightarrow \text{Correction}
\rightarrow a_1 \rightarrow g_1
\rightarrow \text{Verification}
\end{equation}

where $q$ represents the user query, $R_0$ represents the initially
retrieved evidence, $a_0$ represents the initial answer, and $g_0$
represents its grounding score.

\subsection{Initial Retrieval}

Given a user query $q$, the retriever searches the knowledge base and
selects the most relevant documents.

The retrieved document set is represented as:

\begin{equation}
D_q = \{d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_k\}
\end{equation}

The retrieved documents are provided to the language model as contextual
evidence.

\subsection{Initial Generation}

The generator receives the query and retrieved evidence and produces an
initial answer:

\begin{equation}
a_0 = G(q,D_q)
\end{equation}

where $G$ represents the language generation model.

\subsection{Grounding Verification}

The grounding verifier evaluates the semantic relationship between the
generated response and the retrieved evidence. Let $a$ denote the
generated answer and $D$ denote the retrieved evidence set. The
grounding score is represented by:

\begin{equation}
g(a,D) = S(a,D)
\end{equation}

where $S(a,D)$ represents the similarity-based evidence alignment
between the generated answer and the retrieved evidence.

For a generated answer and its retrieved evidence, the verifier
computes a semantic similarity score between the corresponding text
representations. A higher score indicates stronger semantic alignment
between the answer and the available evidence, whereas a lower score
indicates weaker alignment.

For the initial response, the grounding score is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
g_0 = g(a_0,D_q)
\end{equation}

where $a_0$ represents the initial generated response and $D_q$
represents the initially retrieved evidence.

The same grounding evaluation procedure is applied to the candidate
corrected response and its retrieved evidence:

\begin{equation}
g_1 = g(a_1,D_1)
\end{equation}

This common scoring procedure allows the framework to compare the
grounding of the candidate correction with that of the previously
accepted response.

The grounding score is used as a relative verification signal rather
than as a direct measure of answer correctness. Therefore, an increase
in grounding indicates improved semantic alignment with the retrieved
evidence, but does not by itself guarantee that the generated answer is
factually correct.

The grounding improvement for a correction is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g = g_1-g_0
\end{equation}

The resulting improvement value is then used by the
verify-before-accept gate to determine whether the candidate response
should replace the previous response.

\subsection{Correction}

If the initial response does not satisfy the configured grounding
condition, the system performs a corrective action.

A corrected query can be generated as:

\begin{equation}
q' = C(q,a_0)
\end{equation}

where $C$ represents the query-correction mechanism.

The corrected query is then used for additional retrieval:

\begin{equation}
D'_q = R(q')
\end{equation}

where $R$ represents the retrieval process.

A candidate corrected answer is subsequently generated:

\begin{equation}
a' = G(q',D'_q)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Verify-before-Accept Gate}

The central contribution of VBA-SCRAG is the verification gate.

The candidate grounding score is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
g' = S(a',D'_q)
\end{equation}

The grounding improvement is:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g = g' - g_0
\end{equation}

The correction is accepted only when:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g \geq \epsilon
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon$ represents the configured improvement margin.

If the condition is satisfied, the candidate response becomes the
current response:

\begin{equation}
a_0 \leftarrow a'
\end{equation}

A candidate correction is accepted when its grounding improvement
is at least the configured minimum improvement margin. With
$\epsilon=0.00$, a candidate is accepted when its grounding does not
decrease relative to the current response.

\subsection{Bounded Correction}

To prevent unlimited iterative correction, VBA-SCRAG uses a maximum
iteration limit $K_{\max}$.

The correction process terminates when:

\begin{equation}
k \geq K_{\max}
\end{equation}

or when the response satisfies the configured grounding condition.

The bounded correction mechanism limits unnecessary computation and
prevents uncontrolled correction loops.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset}

The preliminary evaluation was conducted using the HotpotQA dataset~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa}
with the \texttt{distractor} configuration. The validation split was
used, and the first 50 questions were selected for the experiment.
For each question, the corresponding gold answer was retained for
reference.

The contextual information provided with each HotpotQA instance was
converted into a document collection by combining each context title
with its associated sentences. Duplicate documents were removed
before constructing the retrieval index. The resulting document
collection was then used by the retriever for all evaluated systems.

The same set of 50 questions and the same document collection were
used across Vanilla RAG, Self-Correcting RAG, and VBA-SCRAG to ensure
a consistent comparison.

\subsection{Compared Systems}

Three configurations were evaluated:

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Vanilla RAG:} retrieves evidence and generates an answer
without performing correction.

\item \textbf{Self-Correcting RAG:} performs a correction step and
automatically accepts the generated correction without the proposed
verification gate.

\item \textbf{VBA-SCRAG:} performs correction followed by explicit
evidence-grounding verification before accepting the candidate
response.

\end{itemize}

All configurations use the same initial dataset, retrieval pipeline,
generator, and grounding measurement procedure wherever applicable.

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}

The experiments were conducted using a fixed experimental configuration
to ensure consistency across the evaluated systems. The retriever uses
the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} sentence-transformer model and retrieves
the top $k=3$ evidence documents for each query. The generator uses
the \texttt{google/flan-t5-small} sequence-to-sequence language model.

The grounding verifier also uses \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} to compute
semantic similarity between the generated answer and the retrieved
evidence. The grounding threshold was set to $\tau=0.75$. When the
current grounding score falls below this threshold, the system
initiates a correction attempt.

The minimum grounding improvement margin was set to
$\epsilon=0.00$. A candidate correction is accepted when its grounding
improvement satisfies:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g \geq \epsilon
\end{equation}

The maximum number of correction iterations was limited to
$K_{\max}=3$. These settings were kept fixed throughout the
experiments.

% ============================================================
% IV. EVALUATION METRICS
% ============================================================

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

The preliminary evaluation measures the behavior of VBA-SCRAG using
evidence-grounding and correction-related metrics. These metrics
evaluate the grounding quality of generated responses, the effectiveness
of the correction gate, and the amount of iterative correction performed
by the system.

\subsubsection{Average Grounding Score}

The primary metric used in the preliminary experiment is the evidence
grounding score. For each evaluated response, a grounding score is
calculated based on the relationship between the generated answer and
the retrieved evidence.

The average grounding score is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
G = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} g_i
\end{equation}

where $N$ represents the number of evaluated questions and $g_i$
represents the grounding score for question $i$.

Both the initial and final grounding scores are calculated to measure
the change produced by the correction process.

\subsubsection{Average Grounding Improvement}

The average grounding improvement measures the change between the
initial and final grounding scores.

It is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
\Delta G = G_f - G_i
\end{equation}

where $G_i$ represents the average initial grounding score and $G_f$
represents the average final grounding score.

A positive value of $\Delta G$ indicates an increase in the average
grounding score, whereas a negative value indicates a decrease.

\subsubsection{Correction Acceptance Rate}

The correction acceptance rate measures the proportion of candidate
corrections that satisfy the configured verification condition and are
accepted by the system.

It is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
CAR =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{accepted}}}
{N_{\mathrm{attempts}}}
\times 100
\end{equation}

where $N_{\mathrm{accepted}}$ represents the number of accepted
corrections and $N_{\mathrm{attempts}}$ represents the total number of
correction attempts.

For the preliminary experiment, 37 of 45 correction attempts were
accepted, resulting in an acceptance rate of:

\begin{equation}
CAR =
\frac{37}{45}\times100
=82.22\%
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Correction Rejection Rate}

The correction rejection rate measures the proportion of candidate
corrections that fail the verification condition and are therefore
rejected.

It is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
CRR =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{rejected}}}
{N_{\mathrm{attempts}}}
\times 100
\end{equation}

where $N_{\mathrm{rejected}}$ represents the number of rejected
corrections.

For the preliminary experiment, 8 of 45 correction attempts were
rejected, resulting in a rejection rate of:

\begin{equation}
CRR =
\frac{8}{45}\times100
=17.78\%
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Average Correction Iterations}

The average number of correction iterations measures the amount of
iterative correction performed by VBA-SCRAG across the evaluated
questions.

It is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{avg}} =
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} k_i
\end{equation}

where $k_i$ represents the number of correction iterations performed
for question $i$ and $N$ represents the number of evaluated questions.

For the preliminary experiment, the observed average number of
correction iterations was 1.22.

These metrics are considered together to evaluate the grounding
behavior of the proposed framework, the behavior of the
verify-before-accept correction gate, and the extent of iterative
processing performed during correction.

\subsection{Correction Gate Behavior}

Across the 50 evaluated questions, VBA-SCRAG generated 45 correction
attempts. Of these attempts, 37 corrections were accepted and 8 were
rejected.

The observed correction behavior is summarized below:

\begin{itemize}

\item Total correction attempts: 45

\item Accepted corrections: 37

\item Rejected corrections: 8

\item Acceptance rate: 82.22\%

\item Rejection rate: 17.78\%

\item Average correction iterations: 1.22

\end{itemize}

The correction acceptance rate is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
CAR =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{accepted}}}
{N_{\mathrm{attempts}}}
\times 100
=
\frac{37}{45}\times100
=
82.22\%
\end{equation}

The correction rejection rate is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
CRR =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{rejected}}}
{N_{\mathrm{attempts}}}
\times 100
=
\frac{8}{45}\times100
=
17.78\%
\end{equation}

The observed rejection cases demonstrate that the correction stage does
not automatically replace the existing response. Instead, the
candidate correction is subject to the configured verification
condition.

\subsection{Correction Acceptance and Rejection}

For each correction attempt $k$, the change in grounding is calculated
relative to the previously accepted response:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g_k = g_k - g_{k-1}
\end{equation}

where $g_{k-1}$ represents the grounding score of the previously
accepted response and $g_k$ represents the grounding score of the
candidate response.

The correction is accepted when the configured verification condition
is satisfied:

\begin{equation}
\Delta g_k \geq \epsilon
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon$ represents the required grounding improvement margin.

If the condition is not satisfied, the candidate correction is
rejected and the previously accepted response is retained.

This verify-before-accept mechanism provides an explicit decision point
between correction generation and response replacement.

\subsection{Threshold Sensitivity Analysis}

To examine the sensitivity of VBA-SCRAG to the grounding threshold,
the threshold $\tau$ was varied while keeping the improvement margin
$\epsilon=0.00$ and the maximum number of correction iterations
$K_{\max}=3$ fixed. The experiment was conducted using the same
50-question HotpotQA validation subset.

The threshold sensitivity results show that varying $\tau$ from 0.60
to 0.90 did not change the average final grounding score for the
evaluated 50-question sample. However, increasing the threshold
resulted in more correction attempts and a gradual increase in the
average number of correction iterations. The selected value
$\tau=0.75$ therefore provides a moderate operating point in the
evaluated configuration, avoiding the lower correction activity
observed with a relaxed threshold while not reaching the higher
correction activity associated with the strictest threshold. These
results should be interpreted as a preliminary sensitivity analysis
rather than evidence that $\tau=0.75$ is universally optimal.

\subsection{Discussion of Results}

The preliminary evaluation shows different behaviors across the three
RAG configurations.

Vanilla RAG maintains its initial grounding score because no correction
is performed. Self-Correcting RAG produces a lower final grounding
score than its initial score in the evaluated configuration, with an
average change of -0.013560.

VBA-SCRAG produces an average final grounding score of 0.354460 from an
initial average of 0.347978, resulting in an average improvement of
0.006482.

The correction gate recorded 45 correction attempts, of which 37 were
accepted and 8 were rejected. The observed acceptance rate was 82.22\%,
while the rejection rate was 17.78\%.

The rejection cases demonstrate the intended behavior of the
verify-before-accept mechanism: generating a correction does not by
itself guarantee that the candidate response replaces the existing
response.

These results constitute a preliminary evaluation based on 50 questions
from the HotpotQA validation dataset. Therefore, the observed grounding
differences should be interpreted within the scope of this experimental
setting. Larger-scale experiments and additional evaluation metrics are
required for a more comprehensive assessment.

% VI. LIMITATIONS
% ============================================================

\section{Limitations}

The current evaluation has several limitations that should be considered
when interpreting the reported results.

First, the experiment uses a subset of 50 questions from the HotpotQA
validation dataset. Although the selected questions provide a
preliminary test of the proposed correction mechanism, this sample is
not sufficient to characterize the behavior of VBA-SCRAG across the
complete benchmark. The observed grounding scores and correction
statistics may therefore vary when evaluated on a larger and more
diverse set of questions.

Second, the evaluation primarily measures evidence grounding rather
than end-to-end answer correctness. A response may obtain a relatively
high grounding score while still failing to provide the exact answer
required by the question. Conversely, a response may contain a correct
answer while receiving a lower grounding score because of limitations
in the automated verification process. Therefore, grounding should not
be interpreted as a complete substitute for answer-level evaluation.

Third, the grounding evaluation depends on the selected verification
mechanism. The verifier provides an automated measure of the alignment
between the generated response and the retrieved evidence. However,
automated verification may not capture every form of factual error,
reasoning error, ambiguity, or unsupported inference. The reliability
of the final decision is consequently influenced by the quality of the
verification model.

Fourth, the current evaluation focuses primarily on grounding
improvement and correction behavior. Additional metrics such as exact
answer accuracy, answer relevance, faithfulness, factual consistency,
retrieval recall, precision, latency, and computational cost should be
included in a more comprehensive evaluation. Human evaluation would
also provide an additional perspective for assessing whether accepted
corrections actually improve the usefulness and correctness of the
responses.

Fifth, the current implementation uses relatively lightweight models
to make the preliminary experiment feasible on the available computing
environment. Larger language models and stronger retrieval and
verification models may produce different correction behavior.
Therefore, the reported results should not be generalized directly to
all model configurations.

Sixth, the correction decision relies on a configured grounding
threshold and improvement margin. A preliminary threshold sensitivity
analysis was conducted on the same 50-question evaluation subset,
showing that varying the threshold from 0.60 to 0.90 changed correction
activity while producing the same observed average final grounding
score. However, a larger-scale sensitivity analysis is required to
determine whether these observations remain consistent across a more
diverse evaluation set.

Seventh, the current experiment does not provide a comprehensive
analysis of computational latency, memory consumption, or the
additional cost introduced by repeated retrieval and generation.
Although the bounded correction mechanism limits the number of
iterations, additional correction attempts can increase computational
requirements compared with a single-pass RAG system.

Finally, the current evaluation does not include a statistical
significance analysis of the differences between the evaluated
configurations. The reported improvement of 0.006482 represents the
observed average difference for the evaluated sample and should
therefore be interpreted as a preliminary descriptive result rather
than evidence of statistically significant improvement. Future
experiments should use larger evaluation sets and appropriate
statistical tests and confidence intervals to determine the robustness
of the observed differences across questions.

% ============================================================
% VII. CONCLUSION
% ============================================================

\section{Conclusion}

This work presented VBA-SCRAG, a verify-before-accept framework for
reliability-aware self-correcting Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The
central objective of the framework is to prevent an automatically
generated correction from replacing an existing response without first
evaluating whether the correction provides measurable improvement in
evidence grounding.

The proposed framework performs retrieval and initial response
generation, followed by evidence-grounding verification. When the
initial response does not satisfy the configured grounding condition,
the framework performs a corrective retrieval and generation step. The
candidate response is then evaluated independently, and its grounding
score is compared with that of the previously accepted response. The
candidate is accepted only when the configured improvement condition is
satisfied. Otherwise, the previous response is retained. A bounded
iteration mechanism is additionally used to prevent uncontrolled
correction loops.

The preliminary evaluation was conducted on 50 questions from the
HotpotQA validation dataset and compared Vanilla RAG,
Self-Correcting RAG, and VBA-SCRAG. Vanilla RAG maintained an average
grounding score of 0.347978, while Self-Correcting RAG produced a final
average grounding score of 0.334418. VBA-SCRAG produced a final average
grounding score of 0.354460 from an initial average of 0.347978,
corresponding to an observed average improvement of 0.006482.

The correction behavior further illustrates the operation of the
verification gate. Across the 50 evaluated questions, 45 correction
attempts were recorded. Of these, 37 candidate corrections were
accepted and 8 were rejected. This resulted in an observed acceptance
rate of 82.22\% and a rejection rate of 17.78\%, with an average of
1.22 correction iterations. These results show that the framework
provides an explicit decision stage between generating a correction and
using that correction as the final response.

An important characteristic of VBA-SCRAG is that correction generation
and correction acceptance are treated as separate operations. A new
response is therefore not automatically considered an improvement
merely because it was generated through an additional retrieval or
generation step. Instead, the candidate is evaluated against the
previously accepted response using the configured grounding criterion.
This provides a controlled mechanism for retaining the existing answer
when the generated correction does not satisfy the required
improvement condition.

The current results are preliminary and should not be interpreted as a
complete evaluation of RAG reliability. The evaluation is limited to a
50-question subset and focuses primarily on evidence grounding.
Nevertheless, the experimental results provide an initial basis for
investigating verification-aware correction as a component of
self-correcting RAG systems.

Future work will extend the evaluation to larger benchmark datasets and
additional question-answering tasks. Further experiments will
investigate stronger language models, retrieval models, and evidence
verifiers. Additional evaluation will also consider answer correctness,
faithfulness, factual consistency, retrieval quality, computational
latency, memory consumption, and statistical significance. Although a
preliminary threshold sensitivity analysis was conducted in the current
study, larger-scale threshold and improvement-margin ablation studies
will be performed to further examine the sensitivity of the correction
gate to its configuration.

Overall, VBA-SCRAG establishes a verification stage between correction
generation and response replacement. This verify-before-accept design
provides a foundation for further research into reliability-aware,
evidence-grounded self-correction for Retrieval-Augmented Generation
systems.
